\title{Timing-Only Estimation and Equalization of\\ Harmonic-Coupled OFDM Channels through\\ Dispersive Time-Modulated RIS}
\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A time-modulated reconfigurable intelligent surface (TM-RIS) that switches its
elements within each OFDM symbol couples every subcarrier to its neighbours
through harmonics of the control sequence. Existing TM-RIS designs treat this
coupling as interference at the receiver, and a recent physically consistent
model shows it is further shaped by the frequency-dispersive response of the
elements. We show that the coupling matrix of each element decomposes exactly
into a sum of Toeplitz matrices, one per switching state, that depend only on
the control timing, multiplied by an input-side response that absorbs the
element dispersion. This yields (i) a pilot-based estimator that needs no
element model, only the control timing, and exploits the low rank of the
two-hop propagation; (ii) an exact matrix-free operator with
$\mathcal{O}(QM\log M)$ cost per product; and (iii) a conjugate-gradient MMSE
equalizer. With two pilot symbols the estimated-channel receiver is within
\TODO{x}\,dB of perfect channel knowledge, whereas the conventional one-tap
receiver saturates at the signal-to-harmonic-interference ratio, and
receivers built on the frequency-flat element model lose up to 6.5\,dB.
\end{abstract}

\section{System Model}
OFDM with $M$ active subcarriers, spacing $\Delta f$, useful duration
$T_u = 1/\Delta f$. Element $q\in\{1,\dots,Q\}$ follows a $K$-slot periodic
control sequence $\{s_q[k]\}_{k=0}^{K-1}$, $s_q[k]\in\{1,2\}$, with period
$T_u$. In state $s$ the element has the single-pole response~\cite{iudice2026}
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_s(f) = -1 + \frac{2\xi_{r,s}}{j2\pi(f-f_{0,s})+\xi_{r,s}+\xi_{i,s}} .
\end{equation}
Under the generalized CP condition~\cite{iudice2026}, the received vector is
$\bm y = \bm H \bm x + \bm w$ with
\begin{equation}
H_{\bar m,m} = \sum_{q=1}^{Q} A^{\rm d}_{q}(\bar m)\, b_q^{[\bar m-m]}(f_c+m\Delta f)\, A^{\rm s}_{q}(m),
\label{eq:H}
\end{equation}
where $A^{\rm s}_q, A^{\rm d}_q$ are the source--element and element--destination
frequency responses and $b_q^{[h]}(f)$ is the $h$-th harmonic of the switched
element response.

\section{Exact Timing--Dispersion Separation}
\textbf{Lemma 1.} For a piecewise-constant $K$-slot sequence,
\begin{equation}
b_q^{[h]}(f) = \sum_{s=1}^{2} t_{q,s}[h]\,\Gamma_s(f),\quad
t_{q,s}[h] = \frac{e^{-j\pi h/K}}{K}\,\mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{h}{K}\right)\!\!\sum_{k: s_q[k]=s}\!\! e^{-j2\pi hk/K}.
\end{equation}
Hence $\bm H = \sum_q\sum_s \bm T_{q,s}\circ\big(\bm a^{\rm d}_q (\bm\Gamma_s\odot\bm a^{\rm s}_q)^{\!\top}\big)$,
where $\bm T_{q,s}$ is Toeplitz and depends \emph{only on the control timing}.
\emph{Proof:} Fourier series of a piecewise-constant periodic function,
grouping slots by state. \hfill$\square$

Consequences: (a) the element dispersion multiplies the input side only and
can be absorbed into a per-state input response, so estimation needs no element
model; (b) each $\bm T_{q,s}\bm x$ is a linear convolution, so $\bm H\bm x$ and
$\bm H^{\mathsf H}\bm y$ cost $\mathcal{O}(QM\log M)$; (c) the harmonic envelope
decays as $1/h$ ($1/h^2$ in energy), so any truncated-band model leaves a
residual that limits estimation and banded equalization.
